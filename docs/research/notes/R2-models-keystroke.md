# R2: pretrained models + keystroke-dynamics literature

Researcher agent, 2026-11-18. Scope: Part A emotion models, Part B embedders, Part C keystroke literature.

**Access limits during this run (read first).** Only `huggingface.co` was reachable (model cards and `/api/models` JSON, fetched with curl). `WebFetch` and every other host (arxiv.org, dl.acm.org, ieee-dataport.org, PMC, Semantic Scholar, Wikipedia) failed DNS, so **Part C rests on web-search result snippets, not on the papers**. Hosts to allow if you want the primary sources checked: `arxiv.org`, `dl.acm.org`, `ieee-dataport.org`, `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`, `www.mdpi.com`, `journals.plos.org`, `www.medrxiv.org`. ## Summary

- **English classifier:** `SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions` (MIT, 125M, 28 GoEmotions labels, ONNX int8 variant exists) is the safest default. Self-reported test macro-F1 0.450 at threshold 0.5, 0.541 with per-label thresholds tuned on the test split (optimistic). ModernBERT/UltraBERT GoEmotions fine-tunes score about 0.49-0.57 but are larger and the 380M one needs `trust_remote_code`.
- **Multilingual / Russian classifier:** `Horizon-Labs/multilingual-emotions-base` or `-small` (Apache-2.0, mmBERT, 28 GoEmotions labels, trained on machine-translated GoEmotions in 35 languages incl. Russian, ONNX included) is the best licence-clean option, but it is a single-source, very new (Oct 2026) model. For Russian only, `fyaronskiy/ruRoberta-large-ru-go-emotions` (355M, MIT, macro-F1 0.48 with tuned thresholds) or the 29M `seara/rubert-tiny2-ru-go-emotions` (macro-F1 0.33) are the options.
- **Armenian:** no emotion classifier found. XLM-R (`hy` in its 100 languages) and mmBERT (`hye` in its 1833-language list) saw Armenian in pretraining, so a fine-tune on our own Armenian data is feasible; zero-shot transfer is untested and should be assumed weak.
- **VAD regression:** only one public checkpoint found, `BenRongey/deberta-v3-base-emobank-vad` (MIT, LoRA adapter, Pearson V 0.774 / A 0.572 / D 0.383, no stated split). Dominance is weak. Plan to train our own head on frozen embeddings + EmoBank.
- **Embedder:** `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` (MIT, 118M, 100 languages incl. hy and ru, ONNX) for the multilingual path; `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (Apache-2.0, 22.7M, English) if English-only speed matters. BGE-M3 (MIT, 568M) is the quality option if CPU time allows.
- **Licence caveats that matter for selling:** the model licence is not the whole story. GoEmotions is Apache-2.0 but is Reddit text (dataset card carries usernames); j-hartmann and xlm-emo-t declare no usable licence and are trained on mixed-licence datasets (treat as `unclear`/`research-only`); tabularisai is trained on LLM-synthetic data and its licence is reported inconsistently.
- **Keystroke signal (honest):** group-level effects of emotion on dwell/latency are small and inconsistent; between-person variance dominates. Expect weak signal that only helps after per-person z-scoring and only for coarse arousal/stress-like states, not discrete emotions. Headline 90%+ accuracies on EmoSurv are almost certainly inflated by within-person splits (my inference).

## Part A: emotion models

### A1. SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions (+ ONNX)

- Source: [model card](https://huggingface.co/SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions), [ONNX card](https://huggingface.co/SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions-onnx), [HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions). `verified-card`.
- Base `roberta-base`; 124,667,678 parameters (API `safetensors.total`). Trained 3 epochs, lr 2e-5, on GoEmotions (multi-label, 28 outputs: 27 emotions + neutral, sigmoid).
- Metrics (self-reported, GoEmotions test split, 5,427 examples, notebook linked from the card): at threshold 0.5 accuracy 0.474, precision 0.575, recall 0.396, F1 0.450. I recomputed the mean of the 28 per-label F1 values from the card table: 0.450, so the headline F1 is **macro-F1**. With per-label thresholds tuned for F1 the card reports precision 0.542, recall 0.577, F1 0.541 (macro); weighted F1 0.611. The tuned thresholds were chosen using the same test split per the card's notebook description ("optimizing the threshold per label"), so 0.541 is optimistic. Weak labels at 0.5: grief 0.000 (support 6), pride 0.000, relief 0.000, annoyance 0.238, realization 0.220. Strong: gratitude 0.919, amusement 0.829, love 0.802.
- Licence: card front matter `license: mit`. Training data: GoEmotions, Apache-2.0 per the [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google-research-datasets/go_emotions) (YAML `license: apache-2.0`); texts are Reddit comments and include usernames (card text: "includes the original usernames of the Reddit users"). Reddit's own terms are a separate legal question for a commercial product (flag for the user; not resolved here).
- Languages: English only.
- ONNX: yes, `onnx/model.onnx` and `onnx/model_quantized.onnx` (int8) in the `-onnx` repo, MIT.
- Independent check: the Horizon-Labs card re-evaluates it on GoEmotions test: macro-F1 0.450 at 0.5, 0.519 with thresholds tuned on validation (the honest number). Source: [Horizon card](https://huggingface.co/Horizon-Labs/multilingual-emotions-base). That is third-party but from a competitor model's author, and single-source.
- Class: **commercial-OK** (model), with the Reddit-data caveat.

### A2. j-hartmann/emotion-english-distilroberta-base (and -roberta-large)

- Sources: [distilroberta card](https://huggingface.co/j-hartmann/emotion-english-distilroberta-base), [roberta-large card](https://huggingface.co/j-hartmann/emotion-english-roberta-large). `verified-card`.
- Base DistilRoBERTa (about 82M per the Horizon table, not confirmed from the API, which gives no safetensors count) / RoBERTa-large. Labels: anger, disgust, fear, joy, neutral, sadness, surprise (Ekman 6 + neutral), single-label softmax.
- Training data: balanced subset of 6 datasets (Crowdflower 2016, Elvis et al. 2018 emotion dataset, GoEmotions, ISEAR, MELD, SemEval-2018 EI-reg), 2,811 per emotion, about 20k total; 80/20 train/eval.
- Metric: "evaluation accuracy is 66% (vs. the random-chance baseline of 1/7 = 14%)", self-reported on the 20% held-out slice of the same balanced mix. Not comparable with GoEmotions numbers. Independent: Horizon table gives BRIGHTER 6-emotion macro-F1 0.297 (single-source).
- Licence: **no licence in the card front matter or the HF tags**. Training datasets have mixed terms (ISEAR and MELD are research-oriented, SemEval is Twitter data; I did not verify each). Class: **unclear**. Do not ship without asking the author or retraining on clean data.
- Languages: English. ONNX: none in the repo (siblings: pytorch_model.bin, tf_model.h5). The card still carries a "working paper will be available soon" placeholder from 2022.

## Part C: keystroke dynamics and emotion

**Evidence quality warning.** Everything below came from search snippets (the paper hosts were unreachable). Numbers are secondary and some conflicted or were missing. Primary-source verification is an open item.

### C1. Epp, Lippold & Mandryk, CHI 2011, "Identifying emotional states using keystroke dynamics"

- Source: [ACM DL entry](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1978942.1979046) (bibliographic data only reached me), plus secondary summaries (`snippet`).
- Design: field study; experience sampling of self-reported emotional states (15 states) tied to keystrokes captured in everyday use; keystroke durations and latencies extracted from a **fixed typing sequence** (secondary descriptions). Classifiers built per state.
- Reported: two-level classification works for 7 of the 15 states (confidence, hesitance, nervousness, relaxation, sadness, tiredness, plus one more in the summary); about 84% for some states (anger, excitement cited as "promising" at 84% in one secondary summary); another later paper says "80% accuracy" for positive vs negative. Sample size: **not confirmed** (one search hit hinted 26 participants in an unrelated attribution; do not use). Whether per-person normalisation was used: not confirmed. Likely limitations (my inference from the design): small sample, fixed text rather than free text, self-labelled states, two-level (high/low) targets rather than discrete emotions.

### C2. Zimmermann, Guttormsen, Danuser & Gomez, 2003

- "Affective computing: a rationale for measuring mood with mouse and keyboard", Int J Occup Saf Ergon 9(4); DOI 10.1080/10803548.2003.11076589 ([record](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_28753)). Feasibility study: SAM and semantic-differential self-ratings, physiology (respiration, pulse), and mouse/keyboard log-file parameters, with users writing a predefined message (secondary description). Sample size, effect sizes and features: **not retrieved**. It is a rationale/feasibility paper, so treat it as design motivation rather than evidence of accuracy.

### C3. Kołakowska & Landowska (Sensors 2021) and Kołakowska (2013 review)

- The 2021 item is a research article, not a review: "Keystroke Dynamics Patterns While Writing Positive and Negative Opinions", Sensors 21(17):5963, [doi.org/10.3390/s21175963](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21175963), [PMC8434638](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8434638/) (`snippet`). Participants recalled and wrote about their most negative and positive learning experiences. About 50 participants (abstract says 50; the public dataset README says 245 vectors = 5 per each of 49 participants; [dataset repo](https://github.com/agatakol/Keystroke-dynamics-patterns-while-writing-positive-and-negative-opinions)). 51 features per vector. Best F1 about 0.76 with an SVM (as cited by a secondary paper); self-reported pleasure/arousal labels gave better results than polarity labels. Per-person normalisation: not confirmed.
- The review closest to the brief's wording is Kołakowska 2013 (HSI conference, pp. 548-555, keyboard/mouse emotion recognition review), and the 2021 review is Yang & Qin, "A Review of Emotion Recognition Methods From Keystroke, Mouse, and Touchscreen Dynamics", IEEE Access 9:162197-162213 ([Northumbria record](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/a-review-of-emotion-recognition-methods-from-keystroke-mouse-and-/)). **The brief's "Kołakowska & Landowska 2021 review" appears to be a mis-citation** (the 2021 Kołakowska-Landowska paper is the Sensors experiment above).

### C4. EmoSurv dataset (Maalej & Kallel, IE 2020)

- Source: [IEEE DataPort page](https://ieee-dataport.org/open-access/emosurv-typing-biometric-keystroke-dynamics-dataset-emotion-labels-created-using) (`snippet`); DOI 10.21227/eae6-pk42; paper "Does Keystroke Dynamics tell us about Emotions? A Systematic Literature Review and Dataset Construction", 2020 16th Int. Conf. on Intelligent Environments, pp. 60-67, DOI 10.1109/IE49459.2020.9155004.
- Content: web-app collected, 124 participants visited and logged data; labels Anger, Happiness, Calmness, Sadness, Neutral; fixed-text and free-text files (free-text file Free Text Typing Dataset.csv, 2.55 MB); free text typed before and after emotion induction; physical keyboards only.
- **Licence: "restricted to non-commercial, educational, and research purposes only, and redistribution is not permitted"** (search summary of the DataPort page; confirm on the page). Download needs a free IEEE login (user action). Class: **research-only**: fine for validating the feature extractor, must not be shipped or used to train a sold model.
- Reported results on it: 93.922% accuracy, kappa 0.9197 with a "Random Committee" classifier ([Maalej, Kallel, Sánchez Medina SSRN 2023 / ULPGC record](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/handle/10553/123120)); Marrone & Sansone (DeLTA 2022, [SciTePress](https://scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2022/113673)) report that about 30-second windows suffice, with deep learning; one snippet claims 33.4% for 5-way recognition from 10 keyboard-only features (source unclear). **Inference:** 5-way accuracy of 94% on a small crowd-sourced set almost certainly comes from random train/test splits that put the same person's samples on both sides (identity leakage), because 5 classes chance is 20% and the literature elsewhere reports weak effects. We should use leave-one-person-out evaluation and not quote these numbers.

### C5. Other work found (2014-2026)

- Lee, Tsui & Hsiao 2014, Biomed Eng Online 13:81, [PMC4091769](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4091769/) (`snippet`): 27 subjects aged 19-27 typed after IAPS picture stimuli; emotion effect significant (p < .001) on keystroke duration, latency and accuracy rate (repeated-measures ANOVA). A companion auditory-stimuli paper ([PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0129056)) found the arousal effect significant for duration and latency but not accuracy. Lab induction, fixed typing, within-subject design; effect sizes not retrieved.
- ACII 2019, "How you type is what you type: Keystroke dynamics correlate with affective content" ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/4534138), `snippet`): participants captioned emotionally rated images (MTurk; "400 participants" in one abstract view, unconfirmed). Image-level mean dwell time correlated **negatively with valence**, and flight time with **arousal**; correlations are at the mean-per-image level (so inflated vs per-message).
- Naegelin et al., 2023 J Biomed Inform (lab, simulated office): mouse+keyboard features best gradient-boosting F1 0.625 (perceived stress), 0.631 (arousal), 0.775 (valence), 3-class (`snippet`, [related page](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/an-interpretable-machine-learning-approach-to-multimodal-stress-d/)); and the 2025 medRxiv field preprint "One does not fit all" ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.02.25332538v1.full)): 8-week field study, N = 36 office employees, keyboard/mouse + cardiac, **personalised models beat one-model-for-all** (leave-one-subject-out for the global model, blocked CV for personalised); the snippet itself says robust field detection remains challenging. Numbers not retrieved. Preprint.
- BiAffect mobile keystroke metadata ([JMIR 2018 e241](https://www.jmir.org/2018/7/e241/PDF), `snippet`): average inter-key delay predicted depression/mania rating scale scores; typing instability predicts future mood outcomes (clinical populations, week-scale state, not message-level emotion). A 2019 abstract (Leow et al.) reports semi-supervised deep learning on touch typing predicting 3-level valence AUC up to 0.84, arousal up to 0.82, dominance up to 0.82 (cross-validated, `snippet`, abstract only).
- The Frontiers in Psychiatry 2025 paper ([link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1430303/full)) and "Smartphone keyboard dynamics predict affect in suicidal ideation" appeared in results but I did not get their numbers.
- Disagreement in the field (a search summary): some groups argue that typing speed, key latency and key duration are only weakly correlated with emotional change; reviews still assert stress affects muscle tension.

### C6. Recommended feature list for engine D (inference, from the literature and the brief's schema)

Principle: **keystroke features are for arousal/stress/effort and deviation-from-baseline, not for discrete emotions.** All features as z-scores against the user's own baseline (rolling median and MAD per feature, per input context if known: desktop vs phone), and fused with a confidence that is low until the baseline has enough messages.

1. Hold (dwell) time: median and IQR, by key category (letters; backspace separately). Literature reports dwell correlating with valence (ACII 2019) and emotion effects on duration (Lee 2014).
2. Flight/inter-key latency (down-to-down): median, IQR, coefficient of variation, log-transformed; also per-digraph latency only once enough personal data exists. Flight correlated with arousal (ACII 2019).
3. Typing speed (characters per second over active typing time, excluding long pauses), and its within-message trend (first vs last third).
4. Pause structure: count of pauses over 1 s, 2 s and 5 s, longest pause, pause-to-active-time ratio, time-to-first-key (hesitation).
5. Backspace/delete rate (deletions per typed character), revision bursts (consecutive deletes), final length vs total typed (net-to-gross ratio).
6. Burst metrics: mean/max number of keys between pauses, inter-burst gap.
7. Rhythm stability: variability of latency (rolling MAD) and "typing instability" as used by BiAffect.
8. Context covariates, used for conditioning rather than as emotion features: message length, time of day, device type, language, fatigue proxy (messages in session). Must be modelled so that "typing slowly because it is 2 a.m." is not read as sadness.
9. Do not log characters, only key categories (brief §4.2); this removes digraph-by-letter features, so use category-pair timings (letter-letter, letter-space, letter-backspace).

Modelling advice: per-person z-scores, then a small regularised model (ridge/logistic) predicting coarse targets (high vs low arousal, high vs low stress, deviation flag), validated leave-one-person-out and, for our own data, by blocked time splits. Report effect sizes with bootstrap CIs per the brief.

### C7. How much signal to expect (honest note)

- Group-level effects exist in controlled lab studies (ANOVA p < .001 with 27 subjects) but are small relative to between-person variation in baseline typing speed; this is why every successful result above either normalises per person, trains personalised models, or leaks identity across the train/test split.
- Field/free-text, per-message accuracy for discrete emotions from timing alone should be expected to be only modestly above chance (my inference). The best-case, honest outcome for our single-user MVP: a small but real correlation between z-scored features and self-reported arousal/stress (plausibly r of order 0.1-0.3) that **adds little beyond the text engines**. The brief's own Phase 5 ablation is exactly the right test; plan the engine as optional with a low fusion weight and a confidence that depends on baseline maturity.
- Do not trust the 90%+ EmoSurv numbers. Use EmoSurv for plumbing validation only.
- Mobile and desktop typing are different regimes; baselines must be per device.
